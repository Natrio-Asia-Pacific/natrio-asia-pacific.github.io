Nest Camera to GCP

Privacy disclosure

How camera information is accessed, processed and controlled within Natrio's private evaluation application.

On this pageCamera accessImage processingStorage and accessYour controlsContact and updates

1. Camera access and purpose

Nest Camera to GCP is operated by Natrio for authorised camera evaluation. It uses Google's Smart Device Management permission, https://www.googleapis.com/auth/sdm.service, to discover authorised devices, inspect their capabilities and establish camera viewing.

The Google permission covers more than a single video operation. The camera account owner should review Google's consent screen and grant access only to the intended devices. Access to the operational viewer requires an approved organisational login.

Camera images can contain people, vehicle registration plates and workplace information. Operators are responsible for using the application only within the approved purpose and relevant workplace permissions.

2. Local analysis and optional cloud review

Continuous detection runs on displayed frames in the operator's browser. That local analysis does not upload frames to an AI service.

An operator can separately consent to selected-image review through Google Cloud Vertex AI. A review request can include selected camera images, a question submitted for image review and a requested visual analysis, such as plate transcription. If the operator also enables automatic candidate review, fresh candidate image crops can be submitted while the page is visible, without a separate click for every request. The interface discloses that processing can occur outside Australia.

The application does not perform facial recognition or identify people by matching their faces.

Google's own services and any optional AI processing remain subject to their applicable terms. This disclosure does not claim that Google retains no information.

3. Storage, access and retention

The application does not record video or persist submitted images, questions submitted for image review, plate text or AI responses. Displayed evidence and detections are transient. Operators may still capture or export information visible in their browser. Separately enabled alarm-rule storage can retain rule instructions; these are distinct from image-review questions.

The OAuth client secret and refresh token are held in Google Cloud Secret Manager and are not exposed to browser users. The application does not need or store the camera owner's Google password. The service retains operational information such as request status and metadata-only usage and cost counters. Google Cloud security and access records may also identify the accounts using the service.

This describes the application, rather than the camera's own recording settings, Google's provider-side retention or the organisation's separately managed records. Stopping a session does not delete records held independently by those systems.

4. Your controls

  • The camera account owner can review permissions and revoke the application's connection in Google's Partner Connections Manager.
  • Operators can stop live viewing and local analysis.
  • Operators can decline optional cloud review, disable automatic candidate review and clear transient review evidence.
  • Access, correction or deletion questions can be sent to the app owner.

Revoking access prevents subsequent camera access. It cannot undo a review request already sent for processing or automatically erase independently retained records.

5. Contact and changes

For support, privacy questions or requests concerning access, correction and deletion, contact cherrera@natrio.com. Natrio's general privacy policy provides wider information about its handling of personal information.

This disclosure describes the application. Changes to Google-data use will be reflected here and, where required, presented to affected users before the changed use begins.

When using or transferring information received from Google APIs, Nest Camera to GCP follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.
